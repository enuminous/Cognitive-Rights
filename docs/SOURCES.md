# Sources and interpretation record

Reviewed 2026-11-18. Primary sources are linked for scrutiny. This is a research
orientation, not a survey of every jurisdiction or advice about an individual claim.

| Source | Relevant passage | Use here and limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Matthew Chenoweth Wright, UDCR (2025-02-01)](https://medium.com/@enuminous/universal-declaration-of-cognitive-rights-1faaa4b9f1e7) | Articles 1–10 and epilogue | Historical normative proposal; archived locally; not evidence of adoption |
| [UN, Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights) | Articles 12, 18 and 19 | Human privacy, thought and expression context; not evidence of AI personhood |
| [OHCHR, International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/ccpr.pdf) | Articles 17–19 | Human privacy, thought and expression protections; application depends on legal context |
| [UNESCO, Recommendation on the Ethics of AI (2021)](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/recommendation-ethics-artificial-intelligence) | Paragraphs 35–36 and 68–69 | Human/legal-person accountability and policy experimentation; important disagreement with the UDCR's proposed equal legal standing |

The UNESCO recommendation places ultimate responsibility with natural or legal persons
and advises against granting AI systems legal personality. This is a policy position,
not proof about consciousness and not a complete account of all current law. We record
that tension openly rather than portraying the UDCR proposal as settled consensus.

No source here derives rights from EFMW equations. Technical verification, moral
argument and legal recognition require different kinds of justification.
